# HF1 High-Flier: field checklist

The parachute is a recovery aid, not the safety system. The range rules below are
what make a total failure harmless. Follow every line on every shot.

## Before you leave home
1. Charge both batteries (A and B) fully. The charge light goes out when they're full.
2. Bench test both channels with the test button: each one heats its cutter for 1 second
   (you'll see the nylon line melt through on a scrap piece).
3. Fit a new nylon holding line. Never reuse one.
4. Inspect the shock cord and parachute: no cuts, no melted spots, lines untangled.

## Choosing the spot
5. Open ground with no people, cars, buildings or roads within 200 m (650 ft) downwind.
6. Know the wind direction. The dart drifts downwind under the parachute.
7. One spotter whose only job is to watch the dart from launch to landing.

## Loading
8. Pack the parachute (fold, roll, lines and cord on top) and slide it in on top of the spring.
9. Fit the nose and tie the holding line through both cutters. Snug, not twanging tight.
10. Switch on channel A, then channel B.
11. Wait for the READY blink on BOTH (pattern listed in the electronics notes).
    No READY on either one: do not launch. Unload and fix it.

## Launch
12. Tilt the cannon 5 to 10 degrees away from everyone, toward open ground. Never straight up.
13. Everyone stands behind the cannon. Nobody downrange, ever.
14. Spotter says "ready", launcher calls "firing", fire.
15. Spotter tracks it all the way down and calls "heads up" if it comes back toward anyone.
    Anyone who hears "heads up" looks up and moves away from the dart's path.

## Recovery
16. Wait until it has landed before walking out.
17. Read the peak height on each board (LED blinks or phone), then switch both off.
18. Write down: date, pressure, dart size, height A, height B, whether the chute opened.

## If the parachute did not open
19. Stop shooting HF1 for the day. Bring the dart home and find out why before the next flight.
